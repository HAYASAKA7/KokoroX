Anime adaptations are interpretations, not one-to-one transfers. A character may feel different because:

- Limited episode time compresses scenes, inner monologue, and gradual development, so subtle traits can become simpler or more exaggerated.
- Directors and writers may adjust characterization to fit the anime’s tone, pacing, target audience, or the role the character plays in the ensemble.
- Reordered events, omitted chapters, and anime-original material can change the context behind a character’s choices.
- Voice acting, music, animation, and localization add emotional cues that may make the same dialogue seem warmer, harsher, funnier, or more dramatic.
- Production, broadcast, marketing, or censorship concerns can lead to softened behavior or greater emphasis on popular traits.

Sometimes the change is deliberate; other times it is an unintended result of translating a long, internally narrated manga into a shorter audiovisual format.
