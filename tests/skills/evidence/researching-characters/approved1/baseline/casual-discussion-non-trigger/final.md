Anime adaptations often change a character’s personality because adaptation is an interpretation, not a direct transcription. A few common reasons are:

- **Limited runtime:** Internal monologues, quiet scenes, and gradual development may be cut, making a nuanced character seem simpler or more extreme.
- **A different medium:** Traits that work subtly on the page may need to be expressed more visibly through dialogue, acting, or recurring jokes.
- **Changes in tone or audience:** Producers may make someone friendlier, funnier, tougher, or less controversial to suit the anime’s target audience and time slot.
- **Staff interpretation:** Directors, writers, voice actors, and editors may emphasize different aspects of the character than the manga’s creator did.
- **Story restructuring:** Reordered, condensed, or anime-original events can change the context behind a character’s actions.
- **An unfinished source:** If the manga is still ongoing, the anime team may not yet know where a character’s arc is heading.

Sometimes the personality has genuinely been rewritten; other times it only feels different because the manga’s narration and inner thoughts are missing. Even voice acting, music, and localization choices can strongly change how the same dialogue comes across.
