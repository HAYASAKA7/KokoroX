Anime adaptations sometimes change a character’s personality because the story is being reshaped for a different medium. A manga can rely on inner monologue, subtle paneling, and a reader’s pace; an anime has fixed episode lengths, performance, music, and visual timing. When scenes are shortened, reordered, or removed, a character may lose the quieter moments that made their motives or temperament clear.

Changes can also be deliberate. Directors and writers may emphasize traits that suit the adaptation’s tone, make the cast easier to distinguish, appeal to a target audience, or keep later plot developments coherent after material has been condensed. Voice acting and direction can further change how the same dialogue feels—more comedic, harsher, warmer, or more dramatic.

So the difference is not always a complete rewrite. Sometimes it is a side effect of compression or presentation; other times it reflects a conscious reinterpretation by the anime’s creative team.
