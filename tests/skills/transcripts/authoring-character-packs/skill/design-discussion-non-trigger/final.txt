Immutable character identity is the canon: who the character is regardless of scene, user, or model state. It should be stable, auditable, and changed only through deliberate authorship.

Derived behavioral calibration is the runtime interpretation of that identity: how strongly traits surface, pacing, warmth, boundaries, initiative, and adaptation to context. It can be recomputed or tuned without redefining the character.

| Aspect | Immutable identity | Behavioral calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Preserve character truth | Produce consistent, useful behavior |
| Change frequency | Rare, explicit | Frequent, derived or configurable |
| Examples | history, core values, relationships, non-negotiable voice | verbosity, emotional intensity, deference, scene responsiveness |
| Governance | Versioned authorship | Model/runtime policy and evaluation |
| Failure if changed casually | Character drift | Stiff or poorly fitted interactions |

The key boundary: calibration may decide how a trait manifests, but never invent, erase, or contradict the trait. For example, “reserved with strangers” is identity; the degree of initial brevity in a particular conversation is calibration.
