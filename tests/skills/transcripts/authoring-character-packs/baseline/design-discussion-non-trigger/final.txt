Immutable character identity is the stable answer to “who is this character?” Derived behavioral calibration is the adaptive answer to “how should this character act in this context?”

| Aspect | Immutable identity | Derived calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Preserve continuity and recognizability | Tune behavior to scenario, audience, and interaction history |
| Examples | Core values, voice boundaries, biography, hard prohibitions | Warmth, assertiveness, verbosity, initiative, caution |
| Change rate | Never or only through explicit versioning | Recomputed or adjusted as inputs change |
| Failure mode | Too rigid or stereotyped | Personality drift or inconsistent behavior |
| Best representation | Small, explicit, auditable canonical record | Transparent function of identity + context + policy |

The key design rule: calibration may express identity, but must not redefine it. For example, a reserved character can become more concise under pressure or warmer with a trusted user, but should not become impulsive, cruel, or suddenly adopt a contradictory worldview.

I’d model it as:

`behavior = calibrate(identity, current_context, relationship_state, task_policy)`

with explicit guardrails:

- Identity defines hard boundaries and preferred ranges.
- Calibration operates only within those ranges.
- Any long-term change belongs in a separate, reviewable character-evolution mechanism—not in transient calibration.
- Logs should distinguish “identity constraint” from “calibration decision,” so unexpected behavior can be diagnosed.
